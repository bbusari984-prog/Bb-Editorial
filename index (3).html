What I Offer

Non-Fiction Editing Services

From a rough first draft to a publication-ready manuscript, every service is tailored to where your non-fiction book is right now.

🧭

Developmental Editing

You know your message. But somewhere between chapter 3 and chapter 9, it stops feeling like a book and starts feeling like a pile of good ideas. This is where I find the real shape of your argument — what stays, what moves, what goes — so a reader can follow you from the first page to the last without getting lost.

Process: I read the full manuscript, then send a detailed editorial letter (4–8 pages) plus chapter-by-chapter margin notes — structural and content feedback, not line-level rewriting.

Why you need it: No amount of beautiful sentences fixes a book whose spine is broken. Get the structure right first, and everything after this gets easier and cheaper.

For you if: you've reread your own manuscript and thought, "this makes sense to me, but would it make sense to someone who's never heard me explain it out loud?"

$0.005 / word
✒️

Line Editing

Your ideas are solid. But when you read your own paragraphs back, something feels flat, or you notice you've used the same transition word five times in one chapter. This is where I work sentence by sentence to make your writing sound like the clearest, most confident version of you.

Process: Edited directly inside your manuscript using Microsoft Word's Track Changes, with comments explaining the reasoning behind each edit, so you can review and accept or reject every single change.

Why you need it: Readers rarely put down a book because the ideas were wrong — they put it down because it was tiring to read. This is the difference between a manuscript people finish and one they don't.

For you if: the argument or story is all there, but you keep thinking "I know what I mean, I'm just not sure I'm saying it well."

$0.01 / word
📝

Copy Editing

You called it "the Framework" in chapter 2 and "the System" in chapter 9. One page says $2.3 million, another says $2.4 million. None of this is a big deal on its own — but a reader's brain notices, and it quietly chips away at how much they trust the rest of the book. This is the pass where I hunt down exactly this kind of thing.

Process: Delivered in Track Changes, alongside a style sheet documenting every spelling, naming, and formatting decision, so nothing drifts between chapter one and the last page.

Why you need it: Credibility is built in small details as much as big ideas. This is what separates a book that reads "professionally produced" from one that reads "rushed."

For you if: you're proud of the message, but no one has gone through it line by line checking that the details actually add up.

$0.005 / word
🔍

Proofreading

Your manuscript is formatted, laid out, maybe already sitting in a print-ready PDF — which is exactly the moment a stray typo becomes permanent and public. This is the last, careful read before the book leaves your hands for good.

Process: Done on the fully laid-out or near-final manuscript, marked up in Track Changes or PDF comments, focused only on catching what's left — not rewriting.

Why you need it: The version people actually buy has to be right. There's no second round once it's published.

For you if: your book is basically done and formatted, and you just want one more trained set of eyes on it before you hit publish.

From $0.008 / word
🗂️

Manuscript Critique

Maybe you're not sure your manuscript is even ready for a full edit. Maybe "is this actually good?" has been running quietly in the back of your mind for months. This is an honest, written answer to that question — before you spend on anything bigger.

Process: I read your manuscript and deliver a written critique letter covering strengths, weaknesses, and a clear, prioritized revision roadmap — no track changes or margin notes, just the assessment.

Why you need it: It gives you clarity and a plan for a fraction of the cost of a full edit, so you're not guessing where to put your time and money next.

For you if: you want the truth about where your manuscript stands, from someone who isn't your spouse, your mom, or your writing group.

$50 – $250 flat (by word count)

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything You Might Want to Ask

Answers to the questions prospective clients ask most before hiring a non-fiction editor.

What does a developmental editor actually do?
A developmental editor evaluates the big-picture elements of your non-fiction manuscript — structure, argument flow, chapter organization, clarity of message, and how well the content serves your target reader. Rather than fixing sentences, a developmental editor helps you fix the book's structure itself, delivering a detailed editorial letter and margin notes before you move to line or copy editing.
What's the difference between developmental editing, line editing, copy editing, and proofreading?
Developmental editing addresses structure, organization, and argument (big-picture). Line editing refines voice, flow, tone, and sentence-level craft. Copy editing corrects grammar, consistency, style, and technical correctness. Proofreading is the final pass that catches remaining typos, spelling, punctuation, and formatting errors before publication. Most non-fiction manuscripts benefit from these in that order.
